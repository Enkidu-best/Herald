Herald {v} - {tag}

Turns a book into an audiobook right on this computer: no internet (except a
one-time download of models), no subscriptions, nothing is sent anywhere.

WHAT IT DOES
  • Reads txt, md, docx, epub, fb2, pdf (text layer; skips scans).
  • Expands numbers, dates, centuries, units and abbreviations in Russian text
    into words with the right grammatical case: "1250 rub." becomes "one
    thousand two hundred fifty rubles" in the case the sentence needs,
    abbreviations are spelled out letter by letter, initials are read as
    letters (ru-normalizr).
  • Places stress by itself: RUAccent; for homographs ("castle" / "lock", spelled the same in Russian) where it
    is unsure - silero-stress. A character's name sounds the same throughout
    the book. “How it will be read” shows the text with stress marks; a wrong
    one can be fixed with a right-click - your fix wins over the automatic one.
  • Splits the book into parts of about the length you set and delivers them
    in order - you can listen to the first ones right in the window (mini
    player) while the rest are computed. Pause, “Continue narration” after a
    stop or a failure, and the Mac doesn't fall asleep mid-book.
  • Pauses are adjustable: after a sentence, after a paragraph and between
    lines of dialogue (0.4 / 0.8 / 1.1 s by default).
  • Word endings at chunk edges aren't lost: each chunk gets a known silence at
    its end, so the model has time to finish the word; the last word is
    checked by its last letters and re-narrated if it fails.

VOICE
  The voice is CLONED from a sample: give it any recording of the narrator you
  want (an audiobook or other connected prose works best) - the app finds a
  clean 7-12 second passage in it, transcribes it (Whisper large-v3-turbo) and
  then reads the book in that voice. Poems, lists, uncertainly recognized
  chunks, and recordings with cut-off highs or clipped waveform peaks are not
  used as samples (only in automatic selection: a voice picked by ear is not
  re-checked).
  There are also fast ready-made voices - no cloning, sound almost at once:
  Silero (built in; CC BY-NC 4.0 license - non-commercial use only), Vosk
  (Apache-2.0) and Piper (CC0 / public domain voices, GPL-3.0 engine) -
  downloaded with a button in Settings.

ENGINES (chosen by blind listening, Settings)
  Main - F5-TTS, Russian model Misha24-10 accent_tune, 12 steps.
  ESpeech-TTS-1 - almost as good, Apache-2.0 license. Draft - faster, but
  noisy. Silero v5 - ready-made voices (non-commercial license).
  English - the lucasnewman/f5-tts-mlx model.
  The app decides by itself what to compute on: a Mac with Apple Silicon - the
  GPU (MLX); Windows and Linux - an NVIDIA GPU (separate download in Settings)
  or the CPU. The algorithm is the same everywhere - so is the sound.

CHECK
  Every chunk is recognized: if an ending is swallowed or a word is missing or
  unclear, the chunk is computed again. The strict check of the whole chunk
  can be turned off (about one and a half times faster).

SPEED
  Apple M4: an hour of sound takes about 1 h 20 min to compute (about 2 h with
  the strict check): good to run a book overnight. NVIDIA GPU - comparable
  (not tested). CPU - a day or more per book; Silero voices are fast there.

WHERE THINGS ARE
  Voices: ~/.cache/text2audio/voices/ (.wav sample, .txt transcript,
  .src.json - which recording it was made from).
  Problem log: ~/Library/Logs/Herald/herald.log (Windows:
  %LOCALAPPDATA%/Herald/Logs, Linux: ~/.local/state/Herald/log)
  Models: ~/.cache/huggingface/
  Result: a folder named after the book, next to the book itself.
